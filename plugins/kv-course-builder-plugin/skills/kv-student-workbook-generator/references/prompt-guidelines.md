# Learner Prompt Guidelines

Use learner prompts to help students engage the source rather than to fill space.

## Good Prompts

Good workbook prompts are:

- tied to the controlling passage or source teaching
- answerable by the intended learner
- concrete rather than vague
- proportionate to the material
- respectful of pastoral sensitivity
- clear about whether the learner is observing, understanding, reflecting, or applying

## Prompt Types

Use only the types that fit the source:

- **Observation:** What does the text or source actually say?
- **Comprehension:** How would you explain this idea in your own words?
- **Comparison:** How are these ideas similar or different?
- **Discernment:** Which statement is better supported by the passage or source?
- **Reflection:** Where does this teaching expose, encourage, or challenge you?
- **Application:** What faithful response is warranted by the source?
- **Prayer response:** How can the learner respond to God in prayer?
- **Discussion:** What would be helpful to process with a group?

## Avoid

Do not use prompts that:

- could fit any passage with no changes
- smuggle interpretation into an observation question
- pressure the learner into forced vulnerability
- turn conviction into shame leverage
- imply prosperity or guaranteed outcomes not supported by the source
- ask too many questions in a row
- require academic knowledge not provided in the workbook
- turn inference or synthesis into direct textual command

## Prompt Count Defaults

- Short worksheet: 3 to 5 total prompts
- Standard workbook page: 5 to 8 total prompts
- Multi-section workbook: 2 to 3 prompts per major section, unless the source requires fewer

Quality matters more than count.
