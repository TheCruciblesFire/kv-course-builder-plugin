# Interaction Decision Rubric

Use this rubric when the interaction decision is not obvious.

## Strong Yes Indicators

Recommend interaction when most of these are true:

- The learner must do more than receive information.
- The desired action is concrete: observe, compare, sort, sequence, discern, diagnose, practice, or self-check.
- Learner friction is real and visible.
- The content can be reduced to a bounded activity without distortion.
- The tone is not trivialized by interaction.
- The source claim is stable enough for learner-facing prompts or feedback.
- The interaction will clarify, reinforce, test, or deepen learning.

## Optional Indicators

Mark interaction optional when:

- A learner action exists, but it is light.
- A reflection prompt or group discussion may accomplish the same purpose.
- The content is useful but does not require answer logic.
- Interactivity may help engagement but is not necessary for understanding.
- The source is sound, but the activity should remain low-complexity.

## No Indicators

Do not recommend interaction when:

- The content is mainly proclamation, prayer, worship, testimony, solemn warning, or pastoral exhortation.
- The material is emotionally weighty and would feel trivialized by a game-like mechanic.
- The proposed activity would add novelty without instructional value.
- The activity would require unsupported answer-key certainty.
- The learner action is unclear.
- The source base is too uncertain to operationalize.
- A simple teaching paragraph, reflection, or leader-guided discussion is better.

## Time, Complexity, and Micro-Learning Fit

When the evaluation concerns a course lesson, Mini Course Generator, student packet, or adult-learning context, include these fields in the output:

- **Estimated time:** Use a modest range, not a precise claim. Example: `3-5 minutes` or `5-7 minutes`.
- **Complexity level:** Use `Low`, `Moderate`, or `High`. Prefer moderate complexity for sorting or claim-support activities with multiple categories. Flag high complexity when answer logic, theological nuance, or platform constraints could burden learners.
- **Adult micro-learning fit:** Use `Strong`, `Moderate`, or `Weak`. Strong fit requires a bounded learner action, concise feedback, and a clear purpose. Weak fit applies when the material requires extended teaching, pastoral processing, or complex theological framing.

Do not inflate complexity to sound sophisticated. The purpose is to help the receiving builder choose an appropriate level of learner demand.

## Conditional Wording for Unsupplied Items

When the final statement list, answer options, card copy, feedback text, or activity items have not been supplied in final form, avoid wording that implies they already exist. Use phrases such as:

- `If included, this statement should be checked...`
- `If the final list contains this claim...`
- `A statement like this would likely be...`
- `Do not finalize this category until the exact wording is reviewed...`

This prevents the evaluator from inventing activity content or sounding as if it has audited unsupplied learner-facing material.

## Common Mechanics and Best Uses

| Format | Best Use | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|
| Guided observation | Help learners notice textual details | It preloads interpretation |
| Sorting | Distinguish categories, claims, or supports | Categories are artificial or disputed |
| Matching | Connect terms, concepts, passages, or definitions | Items require nuance beyond pairs |
| Sequencing | Trace movement, process, events, or argument flow | Order is speculative or unimportant |
| Discernment scenario | Practice wise judgment or application | Scenario becomes manipulative or culturally loaded |
| Self-check card | Review key ideas | Content is too solemn or complex |
| Reflection prompt | Personal response, prayer, conviction, worship | User needs objective skill practice |
| Group discussion | Shared discernment or pastoral processing | A private response is safer |
| Claim-support check | Compare claims with passage support | Passage support has not been reviewed |
| No interaction | Teaching, proclamation, worship, solemn response | Active practice is clearly needed |

## Tone Risks

Prefer reflection, prayer, discussion, or leader guidance when the content involves:

- repentance
- grief or suffering
- confession
- trauma-adjacent stories
- pastoral correction
- sacred worship response
- sensitive family, sexuality, abuse, mental health, or addiction issues
- emotionally intense testimony

## Scripture-Based Activity Risks

Flag and route to passage-faithfulness review when:

- observation prompts imply the intended interpretation
- feedback marks a debated interpretation as the only answer
- biblical-theological synthesis is treated as the local passage's direct claim
- application statements become promises, formulas, or moral burdens
- original-language or background claims become answer logic without support

## Output Boundary

This rubric supports a decision. It does not authorize full activity generation, card copy, answer keys, facilitator notes, LMS packaging, or embed code.

When the recommendation is Yes but required inputs or checks remain, explicitly say not to build the learner-facing activity yet.
